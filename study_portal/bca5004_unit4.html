1. Direct vs Iterative Methods
🎯 Learning Objectives:
  • Distinguish between Direct Methods (exact in finite steps) and Iterative Methods (successive approximations).
  • Understand why partial pivoting is critical in computer implementations of Gaussian Elimination.
Feature Direct Methods (Gauss Elimination, Jordan) Iterative Methods (Gauss-Seidel, Jacobi)
Mechanism Transforms system into upper triangular form via row operations in a fixed number of steps Starts from initial guess vector and refines values in repeated cycles
Round-off Errors Accumulate heavily; can destroy accuracy for large matrices Self-correcting; round-off errors do not compound indefinitely
Best Suited For Dense, small-to-medium matrices ($N < 100$) Large, sparse systems (e.g. electrical networks, structural analysis with thousands of variables)
2. Gauss Elimination with Partial Pivoting
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2021, 2018 (Forward elimination, partial pivoting rationale, and 10-mark solved system)
⚡ Essential Pass Content

Two-Phase Procedure:

  1. Phase 1: Forward Elimination: Transform augmented matrix $[A | B]$ into an Upper Triangular Matrix where all entries below the main diagonal are zero.
  2. Phase 2: Back Substitution: Solve for the last variable $x_n$, then substitute backwards to obtain $x_{n-1}, \dots, x_1$.
⚠️ Why is Partial Pivoting Necessary?
If the diagonal pivot element $a_{ii} \approx 0$, dividing by it to create multipliers causes division by zero or severe floating-point round-off catastrophe. In Partial Pivoting, before eliminating column $i$, we search rows below row $i$ for the largest entry in absolute value $|a_{ki}|$ and swap row $i$ with row $k$.
📝 Solved University Numerical:
Solve the linear system by Gauss Elimination: $$\begin{aligned} 2x + y + z &= 10 \\ 3x + 2y + 3z &= 18 \\ x + 4y + 9z &= 16 \end{aligned}$$

Step-by-Step Augmented Matrix Elimination:

Step 1: Write Augmented Matrix $[A | B]$:
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 & | & 10 \\ 3 & 2 & 3 & | & 18 \\ 1 & 4 & 9 & | & 16 \end{bmatrix}$$
Step 2: Eliminate $x$ from Row 2 and Row 3:
Row operations: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - \frac{3}{2} R_1$ and $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - \frac{1}{2} R_1$:
$R_2$: $[3 - 3, 2 - 1.5, 3 - 1.5 | 18 - 15] \implies [0, 0.5, 1.5 | 3]$
$R_3$: $[1 - 1, 4 - 0.5, 9 - 0.5 | 16 - 5] \implies [0, 3.5, 8.5 | 11]$
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 & | & 10 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 1.5 & | & 3 \\ 0 & 3.5 & 8.5 & | & 11 \end{bmatrix}$$
Step 3: Eliminate $y$ from Row 3 (Using Partial Pivoting):
Notice that $|3.5| > |0.5|$. Swap $R_2$ and $R_3$ for numerical stability: $$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 & | & 10 \\ 0 & 3.5 & 8.5 & | & 11 \\ 0 & 0.5 & 1.5 & | & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ Now eliminate: $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - \frac{0.5}{3.5} R_2 = R_3 - \frac{1}{7} R_2$:
$8.5 \times \frac{1}{7} = \frac{17}{14} \approx 1.2143 \implies 1.5 - 1.2143 = 0.2857 = \frac{2}{7}$
$11 \times \frac{1}{7} = \frac{11}{7} \implies 3 - \frac{11}{7} = \frac{10}{7} \approx 1.4286$
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 & | & 10 \\ 0 & 3.5 & 8.5 & | & 11 \\ 0 & 0 & 2/7 & | & 10/7 \end{bmatrix}$$
Step 4: Back Substitution:
From Row 3: $\frac{2}{7} z = \frac{10}{7} \implies \mathbf{z = 5}$
From Row 2: $3.5 y + 8.5(5) = 11 \implies 3.5 y + 42.5 = 11 \implies 3.5 y = -31.5 \implies \mathbf{y = -9}$
From Row 1: $2x + (-9) + 5 = 10 \implies 2x - 4 = 10 \implies 2x = 14 \implies \mathbf{x = 7}$
Verification: $2(7) + (-9) + 5 = 14 - 9 + 5 = 10$ ✓. Exact integers obtained!
3. Gauss-Seidel Iteration Method
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2023–24, 2020, 2017 (Strict diagonal dominance condition & iterative numerical)
⚡ Essential Pass Content
Strict Diagonal Dominance Condition (Criterion for Guaranteed Convergence):
A system of linear equations is strictly diagonally dominant if in every row, the absolute value of the diagonal coefficient is strictly greater than the sum of the absolute values of the other coefficients in that row: $$|a_{ii}| > \sum_{j \ne i} |a_{ij}| \quad \text{for all } i = 1, 2, \dots, n$$

Key Difference from Gauss-Jacobi: In the Gauss-Jacobi method, all updated values are held until the entire iteration finishes. In the Gauss-Seidel method, as soon as an updated variable (e.g. $x^{(k+1)}$) is calculated, it is immediately used to compute the remaining variables ($y^{(k+1)}, z^{(k+1)}$) in the same iteration! This makes Gauss-Seidel converge twice as fast!

📝 Solved University Numerical:
Solve by Gauss-Seidel Method (perform 3 iterations): $$\begin{aligned} 27x + 6y - z &= 85 \\ 6x + 15y + 2z &= 72 \\ x + y + 54z &= 110 \end{aligned}$$

Step-by-Step Iteration Table:

1. Check Diagonal Dominance:
Row 1: $|27| = 27 > |6| + |-1| = 7$ (Dominant ✓)
Row 2: $|15| = 15 > |6| + |2| = 8$ (Dominant ✓)
Row 3: $|54| = 54 > |1| + |1| = 2$ (Dominant ✓)
The system is strictly diagonally dominant; convergence is guaranteed.
2. Express Each Variable in Iterative Form:
$$x^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{27} \Big( 85 - 6y^{(k)} + z^{(k)} \Big)$$ $$y^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{15} \Big( 72 - 6x^{(k+1)} - 2z^{(k)} \Big)$$ $$z^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{54} \Big( 110 - x^{(k+1)} - y^{(k+1)} \Big)$$
3. Start with $y^{(0)} = 0, z^{(0)} = 0$:
Iteration $x = \frac{85 - 6y + z}{27}$ $y = \frac{72 - 6x - 2z}{15}$ $z = \frac{110 - x - y}{54}$
Iter 1 $\frac{85 - 0 + 0}{27} = \mathbf{3.1481}$ $\frac{72 - 6(3.1481) - 0}{15} = \mathbf{3.5407}$ $\frac{110 - 3.1481 - 3.5407}{54} = \mathbf{1.9132}$
Iter 2 $\frac{85 - 6(3.5407) + 1.9132}{27} = \mathbf{2.4322}$ $\frac{72 - 6(2.4322) - 2(1.9132)}{15} = \mathbf{3.5720}$ $\frac{110 - 2.4322 - 3.5720}{54} = \mathbf{1.9258}$
Iter 3 $\frac{85 - 6(3.5720) + 1.9258}{27} = \mathbf{2.4257}$ $\frac{72 - 6(2.4257) - 2(1.9258)}{15} = \mathbf{3.5729}$ $\frac{110 - 2.4257 - 3.5729}{54} = \mathbf{1.9260}$

After 3 iterations, values stabilize at: $x \approx 2.43, y \approx 3.57, z \approx 1.93$.
