Unit IV: Solution of Linear Equations

Gauss Elimination Method, Partial Pivoting, Gauss-Seidel Iterative Method, and Diagonal Dominance Criterion.

⚡ Essential Concept & Condition Sheet
1. Diagonal Dominance (Condition for Gauss-Seidel Convergence):
A system $AX = B$ converges under Gauss-Seidel if the coefficient matrix is diagonally dominant: $$|a_{ii}| \ge \sum_{j \ne i} |a_{ij}| \quad \text{for all } i$$ (The magnitude of the diagonal element in each row must be greater than or equal to the sum of the magnitudes of all other elements in that row)
2. Gauss-Seidel Iteration Formulae:
$$x^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{a_{11}} \left[ b_1 - a_{12}y^{(k)} - a_{13}z^{(k)} \right]$$ $$y^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{a_{22}} \left[ b_2 - a_{21}\mathbf{x^{(k+1)}} - a_{23}z^{(k)} \right] \quad \text{(Uses newest } x^{(k+1)}\text{)}$$ $$z^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{a_{33}} \left[ b_3 - a_{31}\mathbf{x^{(k+1)}} - a_{32}\mathbf{y^{(k+1)}} \right] \quad \text{(Uses newest } x^{(k+1)}, y^{(k+1)}\text{)}$$
1. Gauss Elimination Method
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24 (Direct method using row operations)

Method Strategy: Converts the augmented matrix $[A | B]$ into an Upper Triangular Matrix using elementary row operations (Forward Elimination), and then finds the unknowns from bottom to top (Back Substitution).

📝 Step-by-Step Solved PYQ: Solve the 3x3 Linear System

Solve the equations:
(1) $x + y + z = 6$
(2) $2x + 3y + z = 10$
(3) $x + 2y + 3z = 13$

Step 1: Form the Augmented Matrix $[A | B]$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & | & 10 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & | & 13 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2: Eliminate $x$ from Rows 2 & 3:
Row Operation: $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$
$\begin{bmatrix} 2-2(1) & 3-2(1) & 1-2(1) & | & 10-2(6) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 & | & -2 \end{bmatrix}$
Row Operation: $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$
$\begin{bmatrix} 1-1 & 2-1 & 3-1 & | & 13-6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 7 \end{bmatrix}$

The matrix becomes:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & | & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Eliminate $y$ from Row 3:
Row Operation: $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$
$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1-1 & 2-(-1) & | & 7-(-2) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 3 & | & 9 \end{bmatrix}$

Upper Triangular Form Achieved!

Step 4: Back Substitution:
From Row 3: $3z = 9 \implies \mathbf{z = 3}$
From Row 2: $y - z = -2 \implies y - 3 = -2 \implies \mathbf{y = 1}$
From Row 1: $x + y + z = 6 \implies x + 1 + 3 = 6 \implies \mathbf{x = 2}$

Final Solution: $\mathbf{x = 2, \quad y = 1, \quad z = 3}$ (Check: $2+1+3 = 6$ ✓)
2. Why Pivoting is Needed?
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2021 (Explain Partial Pivoting with reason)
  • Division by Zero: If the pivot element $a_{kk} = 0$, the multiplier $m = a_{ik}/a_{kk}$ cannot be computed.
  • Catastrophic Round-off Errors: If a pivot element $|a_{kk}|$ is very close to zero, dividing by it generates massive multipliers, which magnifies rounding errors and leads to completely false answers.
  • Partial Pivoting Solution: At step $k$, find the row $p \ge k$ that has the maximum absolute value in column $k$, i.e., $\max |a_{ik}|$, and swap Row $k$ with Row $p$ before eliminating.
3. Gauss-Seidel Iterative Method
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24 (Successive replacement method)
📝 Step-by-Step Solved PYQ: Solve by Gauss-Seidel Method

Solve the system:
(1) $10x + y + z = 12$
(2) $2x + 10y + z = 13$
(3) $2x + 2y + 10z = 14$

Step 1: Check Diagonal Dominance:
Row 1: $|10| > |1| + |1| = 2$ (Dominant! ✓)
Row 2: $|10| > |2| + |1| = 3$ (Dominant! ✓)
Row 3: $|10| > |2| + |2| = 4$ (Dominant! ✓)
Convergence is guaranteed!

Step 2: Express variables:
$x = \frac{12 - y - z}{10}$
$y = \frac{13 - 2x - z}{10}$
$z = \frac{14 - 2x - 2y}{10}$

Start with initial guess $y^{(0)} = 0, z^{(0)} = 0$:

Iter ($k$) $x^{(k)} = \frac{12 - y - z}{10}$ $y^{(k)} = \frac{13 - 2x - z}{10}$ $z^{(k)} = \frac{14 - 2x - 2y}{10}$
1 $\frac{12 - 0 - 0}{10} = \mathbf{1.2000}$ $\frac{13 - 2(1.2) - 0}{10} = \mathbf{1.0600}$ $\frac{14 - 2(1.2) - 2(1.06)}{10} = \mathbf{0.9480}$
2 $\frac{12 - 1.06 - 0.948}{10} = \mathbf{0.9992}$ $\frac{13 - 2(0.9992) - 0.948}{10} = \mathbf{1.0054}$ $\frac{14 - 2(0.9992) - 2(1.0054)}{10} = \mathbf{0.9991}$
3 $\frac{12 - 1.0054 - 0.9991}{10} = \mathbf{0.9996}$ $\frac{13 - 2(0.9996) - 0.9991}{10} = \mathbf{1.0002}$ $\frac{14 - 2(0.9996) - 2(1.0002)}{10} = \mathbf{1.0000}$
Final Converged Solution: $\mathbf{x \approx 1.000, \quad y \approx 1.000, \quad z \approx 1.000}$.
4. Gauss-Jacobi vs Gauss-Seidel Method
Feature Gauss-Jacobi Method Gauss-Seidel Method
Value Usage Uses only old values from previous iteration $(k)$ Uses newest computed values $(k+1)$ immediately
Rate of Convergence Slow convergence Twice as fast as Jacobi
Memory Requirements Needs two separate vectors (old & new) Overwrites in-place (Needs only 1 vector)
