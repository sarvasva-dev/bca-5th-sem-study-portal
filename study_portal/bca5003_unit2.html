Unit II: Transmission Media & Channel Capacity

Guided Media (UTP, STP, Coax, Optical Fiber), Unguided Wireless (Radio, Microwave, Infrared), Transmission Impairments, Nyquist & Shannon Limits.

⚡ Essential Channel Capacity Formulae
1. Nyquist Bit Rate (Noiseless Channel):
$$C = 2B \log_2(M) \quad \text{bps}$$ Where $B = \text{Bandwidth in Hz}$, $M = \text{Number of discrete signal levels}$.
2. Shannon Capacity Theorem (Noisy Channel):
$$C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR}) \quad \text{bps}$$ Where $\text{SNR} = \text{Signal-to-Noise Ratio (Linear)}$.
$$\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(\text{SNR}) \iff \text{SNR} = 10^{\frac{\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}}}{10}}$$
3. Signal Attenuation in Decibels:
$$\text{dB} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)$$
1. Optical Fiber Cable & Working Principle
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2018, 2014 (Structure, working principle, advantages over copper)

Optical fiber transmits data as pulses of light through thin strands of ultra-pure glass or silica based on the principle of Total Internal Reflection (TIR).

Total Internal Reflection: $n_1 > n_2$ and angle of incidence $\theta > \theta_c$
Two Essential Conditions for Total Internal Reflection:
  1. The refractive index of the Core ($n_1$) must be strictly greater than that of the Cladding ($n_2$), i.e., $n_1 > n_2$.
  2. The angle of incidence ($\theta$) at the core-cladding boundary must be greater than the critical angle $\theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)$.

2. The 3 Transmission Impairments
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2022–23, 2021 (Explain Attenuation, Distortion & Noise with diagrams)
  • 1. Attenuation (Loss of Energy): As a signal propagates through any medium, electrical resistance or absorption converts signal power into heat, reducing amplitude. Restored using amplifiers or repeaters.
  • 2. Distortion (Change of Shape): Composite signals consist of different frequency components. In physical media, each frequency travels at a slightly different propagation speed. They arrive at different times (phase shift), distorting the waveform.
  • 3. Noise (External Corruption): Unwanted signals injected from external sources:
    • Thermal Noise: Random movement of electrons in conductors (unavoidable).
    • Induced Noise: Created by motors, power lines, and appliances acting as transmitting antennas.
    • Crosstalk: Effect of one wire on an adjacent wire (capacitive/inductive coupling).
    • Impulse Noise: Sudden high-energy spikes caused by lightning or power surges.
3. Solved PYQ: Shannon Channel Capacity
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2021, 2017 (Standard Numerical Problem)
📝 Solved Problem: Bandwidth = 3000 Hz, SNR = 3162 (or 35 dB)

Given: A standard telephone line has Bandwidth $B = 3000\text{ Hz}$ and $\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 35\text{ dB}$. Find maximum theoretical channel capacity $C$.

Step 1: Convert SNR from dB to Linear Ratio:
$$\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10 \log_{10}(\text{SNR}) = 35$$ $$\log_{10}(\text{SNR}) = 3.5 \implies \text{SNR} = 10^{3.5} \approx \mathbf{3162.28}$$

Step 2: Apply Shannon's Capacity Formula:
$$C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR}) = 3000 \times \log_2(1 + 3162.28) = 3000 \times \log_2(3163.28)$$

Using $\log_2(x) = \frac{\log_{10}(x)}{\log_{10}(2)} = \frac{3.5001}{0.3010} \approx \mathbf{11.628}$ bits:

$$C = 3000 \times 11.628 \approx \mathbf{34,884} \text{ bps} \approx \mathbf{34.88} \text{ kbps}$$

Result: The theoretical data limit of this telephone channel is approximately 34.88 kbps.
