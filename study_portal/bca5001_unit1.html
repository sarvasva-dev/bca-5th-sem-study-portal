Unit I: Business Intelligence & Decision Modeling

Modeling Decision Process, Decision Support Systems (DSS), Group Decision Support Systems (GDSS), and Groupware Technologies.

1. Modeling the Decision Process
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2023–24 (Explain steps involved in decision-making process)

Decision modeling is the structured representation of a real-world decision problem. The classic foundation is Herbert Simon's 4-Phase Decision Model:

Herbert Simon's Decision Making Model: Intelligence → Design → Choice → Implementation
The 4 Phases Explained:
  1. Intelligence: Identify and define the problem or opportunity. Collect raw organizational data and environmental conditions.
  2. Design: Formulate, develop, and analyze possible alternative courses of action. Construct models to predict outcomes.
  3. Choice: Evaluate and compare all generated alternatives using decision criteria. Select the single best solution.
  4. Implementation: Put the chosen decision into action, allocate resources, and establish monitoring controls.
2. Decision Support Systems (DSS)
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2023–24, 2019, 2017, 2016 (Concept, 4 components, and role in business)

A Decision Support System (DSS) is a computer-based information system that assists managers in making decisions for semi-structured and unstructured problems by combining data, sophisticated analytical models, and user-friendly software.

DSS Subsystem Component Role & Description
1. Data Management Subsystem Stores and manages organizational internal databases (ERP, CRM) and external market data via a dedicated DSS Database.
2. Model Management Subsystem Contains mathematical, financial, forecasting, and statistical models in a Model Base to evaluate scenarios.
3. Knowledge-Base Subsystem Provides specialized domain problem-solving rules and artificial intelligence heuristics.
4. User Interface (Dialog) Subsystem Enables intuitive human-computer interaction through charts, dashboards, and scenario inputs.
Key Analytical Capabilities of DSS:
• What-If Analysis: Observes how outcomes change when specific input variables are modified (e.g. "What if advertising budget increases by 15%?").
• Goal-Seeking Analysis: Calculates the input values necessary to achieve a desired target outcome (e.g. "What sales volume is required to achieve 20 Lakhs profit?").
• Sensitivity Analysis: Determines which parameters have the greatest impact on the final decision.
3. Group Decision Support Systems (GDSS)
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2023–24, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2017, 2016 (Super high frequency!)

A GDSS is an interactive computer-based system that facilitates the solution of unstructured problems by a group of decision-makers working together as a team.

Parameter DSS (Individual) GDSS (Group / Team)
Primary User Individual manager or analyst Multiple decision-makers working collaboratively
Key Focus Individual optimization & modeling Consensus building, voting & communication
Distinct Capabilities What-if, sensitivity analysis Anonymity, electronic brainstorming, idea ranking
Key Components Data, Model, UI Hardware, Group Software, Facilitator, Meeting Room
Why Anonymity in GDSS is Crucial:
In traditional meetings, junior members hesitate to criticize senior executives' ideas due to fear of career reprisal. GDSS supports anonymous voting and brainstorming, so ideas are evaluated purely on merit rather than who proposed them, eliminating conformity pressure and groupthink!
4. Groupware Technologies & Time/Space Matrix
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2020, 2019, 2018, 2014 (Define groupware and asynchronous tools)

Groupware refers to collaboration software designed to enable groups of people to share information, coordinate tasks, and work together over a network.

Type Timing Tools & Technologies Use Cases
Synchronous Groupware Same Time (Real-time collaboration) Video conferencing (Zoom, Google Meet), Instant messaging / chat, shared digital whiteboards, screen sharing Live meetings, virtual emergency war-rooms, pair coding
Asynchronous Groupware Different Times (Time-shifted collaboration) Email, threaded discussion forums, Google Docs / Wiki shared authoring, workflow routing, shared document repositories Global teams across time-zones, project tracking, official document approval
5. The 4 Levels of Business Analytics
  • 1. Descriptive Analytics ("What happened?"): Analyzes historical data through dashboards, summaries, and monthly reports.
  • 2. Diagnostic Analytics ("Why did it happen?"): Drills down into data anomalies to locate root causes (e.g., sales dropped due to supply stockout).
  • 3. Predictive Analytics ("What is likely to happen?"): Uses statistical models and machine learning to forecast future trends (e.g., predicting customer churn).
  • 4. Prescriptive Analytics ("What action should be taken?"): Recommends specific optimization strategies and automated actions to maximize business objectives.
