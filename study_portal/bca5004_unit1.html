Unit I: Roots of Equations

Bisection Method, Regula-Falsi (False Position), Newton-Raphson Method, Quadratic Rate of Convergence, and Descartes' Rule of Signs.

⚡ Essential Formula Cheat Sheet
1. Bisection Method Midpoint:
$$c = \frac{a + b}{2}$$ Condition: $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$. Max error after $n$ steps: $\frac{b-a}{2^{n+1}}$.
2. Regula-Falsi (False Position) Formula:
$$x = \frac{a f(b) - b f(a)}{f(b) - f(a)} = a - \frac{f(a)(b-a)}{f(b) - f(a)}$$
3. Newton-Raphson Iteration Formula:
$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$$ Condition: $f'(x_n) \ne 0$. Rate of convergence = Quadratic ($p = 2$).

1. Bisection Method
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24, 2021, 2018 (Asked repeatedly!)

Core Concept: If a continuous function $f(x)$ changes sign over $[a, b]$, i.e., $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$, by Intermediate Value Theorem, at least one real root exists in $(a, b)$. The Bisection Method repeatedly cuts this interval exactly in half.

Standard 7-Step Algorithm (Write this for 10 Marks):
  1. Express the equation as $f(x) = 0$.
  2. Find two values $a$ and $b$ such that $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$.
  3. Calculate the midpoint $c = \frac{a + b}{2}$.
  4. Evaluate $f(c)$. If $f(c) == 0$, $c$ is the exact root!
  5. If $f(a) \cdot f(c) < 0$, set $b = c$ (root lies in left half).
  6. Else set $a = c$ (root lies in right half).
  7. Repeat steps 3–6 until $|b - a| < \text{tolerance}$ or desired decimal accuracy is achieved.
📝 Step-by-Step Solved PYQ: Find root of $x^3 - x - 11 = 0$

Let $f(x) = x^3 - x - 11$.

Test integers:
$f(2) = 2^3 - 2 - 11 = 8 - 13 = -5 < 0$
$f(3) = 3^3 - 3 - 11 = 27 - 14 = +13 > 0$
Since $f(2) \cdot f(3) < 0$, root lies in [2, 3].

2. Newton-Raphson Method & Derivation
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24, 2021 (Derivation + Proof of Quadratic Convergence)
📐 Derivation of Formula from Taylor Series

Let $x_n$ be an approximation to the true root $\alpha$ such that $\alpha = x_n + h$, where $h$ is a small error correction.

Since $f(\alpha) = 0$, we have $f(x_n + h) = 0$.

Expanding $f(x_n + h)$ by Taylor's Series:

f(x_n + h) = f(x_n) + h f'(x_n) + \frac{h^2}{2!} f''(x_n) + \dots = 0

Neglecting $h^2$ and higher order terms (since $h$ is very small):

f(x_n) + h f'(x_n) \approx 0 \implies h \approx -\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}

Since $x_{n+1} = x_n + h$, we substitute $h$:

$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$$
🔥 Proof of Quadratic Rate of Convergence ($p = 2$)

Let $\alpha$ be the true root ($f(\alpha) = 0$) and $\epsilon_n = x_n - \alpha$ be the error at step $n$.

Then $x_n = \alpha + \epsilon_n$ and $x_{n+1} = \alpha + \epsilon_{n+1}$.

From the Newton-Raphson formula:

\alpha + \epsilon_{n+1} = \alpha + \epsilon_n - \frac{f(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}{f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}

Expanding by Taylor series about $\alpha$ (noting $f(\alpha) = 0$):

f(\alpha + \epsilon_n) = \epsilon_n f'(\alpha) + \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2} f''(\alpha) + \dots

f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n) = f'(\alpha) + \epsilon_n f''(\alpha) + \dots

Dividing and simplifying gives:

$$\epsilon_{n+1} \approx \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2 f'(\alpha)} \epsilon_n^2 \implies \epsilon_{n+1} \propto \epsilon_n^2$$

Conclusion: The error at step $n+1$ is proportional to the square of the error at step $n$. Thus, the rate of convergence is quadratic (order 2), meaning the number of correct decimal places roughly doubles with every single iteration!

📝 Step-by-Step Solved PYQ: Solve $x^3 - x - 1 = 0$ with $x_0 = 1.5$

3. Regula-Falsi vs Bisection vs Newton-Raphson
Feature Bisection Regula-Falsi Newton-Raphson
Type Bracketing Bracketing (Chord) Open (Tangent)
Order of Convergence 1 (Linear) 1 (Linear) 2 (Quadratic)
Guaranteed Convergence? Yes (Always) Yes (Always) No (Fails if $f'(x) \approx 0$)
Requires Derivative? No No Yes ($f'(x)$)
Formula $\frac{a+b}{2}$ $\frac{a f(b) - b f(a)}{f(b) - f(a)}$ $x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$
4. Descartes' Rule of Signs

Rule 1 (Positive Real Roots): The number of positive real roots of a polynomial $P(x) = 0$ cannot exceed the number of sign changes in the coefficients of $P(x)$.

Rule 2 (Negative Real Roots): The number of negative real roots cannot exceed the number of sign changes in $P(-x)$.

Example: For $P(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x - 1 = 0$:
Signs in $P(x)$: $+ , - , - , -$ (Only 1 sign change) $\implies$ Exactly 1 positive real root.
Signs in $P(-x) = -x^3 - x^2 + x - 1$: $- , - , + , -$ (2 sign changes) $\implies$ At most 2 negative roots.
