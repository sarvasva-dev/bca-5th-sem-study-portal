1. Core Concepts: Roots & Bracketing Methods
🎯 Learning Objectives:
  • Define what constitutes a root of an equation $f(x) = 0$.
  • Understand the Intermediate Value Theorem (Bolzano's Theorem).
  • Distinguish between Bracketing (Closed) Methods and Open Methods.
💡 Everyday Intuition: Think of finding a root like tracking where a roller coaster dips below ground level. If the coaster is at height $+10$ meters at time $t=1$ and at $-5$ meters at time $t=2$, it must have crossed ground level (height $0$) somewhere between $t=1$ and $t=2$. That crossing point is the root!

Formal Definition: A number $\alpha$ is called a root or zero of an equation $f(x) = 0$ if and only if $f(\alpha) = 0$. In analytical algebra, linear and quadratic equations have exact closed-form solutions (like the quadratic formula). However, transcendental and higher-order polynomial equations (e.g., $x^3 - 4x - 9 = 0$ or $x e^x = 1$) cannot be solved analytically. We must use iterative numerical methods to approximate roots to any desired accuracy.

Classification Method Name Initial Guesses Needed Guaranteed to Converge?
Bracketing (Closed) Bisection, Regula-Falsi Two points $a, b$ such that $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$ Yes (Always converges if $f$ is continuous)
Open Methods Newton-Raphson, Secant One or two points without sign condition No (Much faster, but can diverge if initial guess is poor)
2. The Bisection Method (Bolzano's Method)
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2022, 2019, 2016 (Frequently asked for 5–10 marks with numerical problem)
⚡ Essential Pass Content
Bisection Iteration Formula:
$$c_n = \frac{a_n + b_n}{2}$$ Condition: $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$. Error after $n$ iterations: $\epsilon_n \le \frac{b - a}{2^n}$.

Step-by-Step Algorithm:

  1. Step 1: Find two initial values $a$ and $b$ such that $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$ (opposite signs).
  2. Step 2: Compute the midpoint: $c = \frac{a + b}{2}$.
  3. Step 3: Evaluate $f(c)$:
    • If $f(c) = 0$, then $c$ is the exact root.
    • If $f(a) \cdot f(c) < 0$, the root lies in $[a, c]$. Set $b = c$.
    • If $f(c) \cdot f(b) < 0$, the root lies in $[c, b]$. Set $a = c$.
  4. Step 4: Repeat until the interval width $|b - a| < \epsilon$ (specified tolerance).
📝 Solved University Numerical (CSJMU Past Exam):
Problem: Find a real root of the equation $f(x) = x^3 - 4x - 9 = 0$ using the Bisection method correct to 2 decimal places.

Detailed Step-by-Step Working:

1. Find bracket: test integer values:
$f(2) = 2^3 - 4(2) - 9 = 8 - 8 - 9 = -9 < 0$
$f(3) = 3^3 - 4(3) - 9 = 27 - 12 - 9 = +6 > 0$
Since $f(2) < 0$ and $f(3) > 0$, a root lies in the interval $[2, 3]$.

📚 Full Understanding Track: Theoretical Properties
Theoretical Analysis of Bisection Method:
  • Rate of Convergence: Linear ($p = 1$). Each step halves the interval, gaining approximately $\approx 0.301$ decimal digits of precision per iteration ($2^{-10} \approx 10^{-3}$, so 10 iterations yield ~3 decimal places).
  • Formula for iterations needed: To achieve accuracy $\epsilon$, we solve $\frac{b-a}{2^n} < \epsilon \implies n > \frac{\ln(b-a) - \ln \epsilon}{\ln 2}$.
  • Limitation: It is slow and cannot determine roots of even multiplicity where $f(x)$ touches the x-axis without changing sign (e.g., $f(x) = (x-2)^2$).
3. Regula-Falsi (Method of False Position)
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2023–24, 2021, 2018 (Derivation of chord formula and numerical comparison with Bisection)
⚡ Essential Pass Content
Regula-Falsi Formula (Chord / Secant Formula):
$$x = \frac{a \cdot f(b) - b \cdot f(a)}{f(b) - f(a)}$$

Geometric Principle: Instead of blindly halving the interval like Bisection, Regula-Falsi connects the points $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$ with a straight line chord. The point where this chord intersects the x-axis ($y = 0$) serves as the next approximation.

📚 Full Understanding Track: Mathematical Derivation
Derivation of the Regula-Falsi Formula:
The equation of the straight line joining $A(a, f(a))$ and $B(b, f(b))$ is given by the two-point slope form: $$y - f(a) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} (x - a)$$ To find the x-intercept, set $y = 0$: $$-f(a) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} (x - a)$$ $$(x - a) = -\frac{f(a)(b - a)}{f(b) - f(a)}$$ $$x = a - \frac{f(a)(b - a)}{f(b) - f(a)} = \frac{a[f(b) - f(a)] - f(a)[b - a]}{f(b) - f(a)}$$ $$x = \frac{a f(b) - a f(a) - b f(a) + a f(a)}{f(b) - f(a)} = \frac{\mathbf{a f(b) - b f(a)}}{\mathbf{f(b) - f(a)}} \quad \blacksquare$$
4. Newton-Raphson Method & Quadratic Convergence
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2020, 2017, 2014 (Derivation, order of convergence proof, and failure conditions)
⚡ Essential Pass Content
Newton-Raphson Iteration Formula:
$$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$$ Condition: $f'(x_n) \ne 0$. Order of Convergence = 2 (Quadratic).

Geometric Principle: From an initial guess $x_0$, construct the tangent line to the curve $y = f(x)$ at $(x_0, f(x_0))$. The point where this tangent line intersects the x-axis gives the next approximation $x_1$.

Newton-Raphson Geometric Tangent: The tangent at (x₀, f(x₀)) strikes the axis at x₁ closer to root α.
📝 Solved University Numerical:
Problem: Compute $\sqrt{12}$ using the Newton-Raphson method correct to 4 decimal places.

Let $x = \sqrt{12} \implies x^2 = 12 \implies f(x) = x^2 - 12 = 0$.
Derivative: $f'(x) = 2x$.
Formula: $$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{x_n^2 - 12}{2x_n} = \frac{2x_n^2 - x_n^2 + 12}{2x_n} = \frac{1}{2} \left( x_n + \frac{12}{x_n} \right)$$

Initial Guess: Since $3^2 = 9$ and $4^2 = 16$, choose $x_0 = 3.5$.
Iteration 1:
$$x_1 = \frac{1}{2} \left( 3.5 + \frac{12}{3.5} \right) = \frac{1}{2} (3.5 + 3.42857) = \mathbf{3.46429}$$
Iteration 2:
$$x_2 = \frac{1}{2} \left( 3.46429 + \frac{12}{3.46429} \right) = \frac{1}{2} (3.46429 + 3.46391) = \mathbf{3.46410}$$
Iteration 3:
$$x_3 = \frac{1}{2} \left( 3.46410 + \frac{12}{3.46410} \right) = \mathbf{3.46410}$$ Notice: In just 2 iterations, the value is identical to 5 decimal places! $\sqrt{12} \approx \mathbf{3.4641}$.
📚 Full Understanding Track: Complete Proof of Quadratic Convergence
Proof of Quadratic Convergence ($p = 2$):
Let $\alpha$ be the exact root so that $f(\alpha) = 0$.
Let $x_n = \alpha + \epsilon_n$, where $\epsilon_n$ is the error in the $n$-th iteration.
Then $x_{n+1} = \alpha + \epsilon_{n+1}$.

The Newton-Raphson formula is:

$$\alpha + \epsilon_{n+1} = \alpha + \epsilon_n - \frac{f(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}{f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}$$ $$\epsilon_{n+1} = \epsilon_n - \frac{f(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}{f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n)}$$ Expanding $f(\alpha + \epsilon_n)$ and $f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n)$ by Taylor's series around $\alpha$ (with $f(\alpha) = 0$): $$f(\alpha + \epsilon_n) = \epsilon_n f'(\alpha) + \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2} f''(\alpha) + O(\epsilon_n^3)$$ $$f'(\alpha + \epsilon_n) = f'(\alpha) + \epsilon_n f''(\alpha) + O(\epsilon_n^2)$$ Substituting: $$\epsilon_{n+1} = \epsilon_n - \frac{\epsilon_n f'(\alpha) + \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2} f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha) \left[ 1 + \epsilon_n \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)} \right]}$$ Using binomial expansion $(1 + z)^{-1} \approx 1 - z$: $$\epsilon_{n+1} \approx \epsilon_n - \left( \epsilon_n + \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2} \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)} \right) \left( 1 - \epsilon_n \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)} \right)$$ $$\epsilon_{n+1} \approx \epsilon_n - \left( \epsilon_n - \epsilon_n^2 \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)} + \frac{\epsilon_n^2}{2} \frac{f''(\alpha)}{f'(\alpha)} \right)$$ $$\epsilon_{n+1} \approx \frac{f''(\alpha)}{2 f'(\alpha)} \epsilon_n^2 = C \cdot \epsilon_n^2$$ Since $\epsilon_{n+1} \propto \epsilon_n^2$, the order of convergence is **2 (Quadratic)**! $\blacksquare$
⚠️ When Does Newton-Raphson Fail? (Critical Exam Theory Question):
  1. Division by Zero: If $f'(x_n) = 0$ (horizontal tangent), $x_{n+1} \to \infty$.
  2. Oscillatory Cycles: If the tangent toggles back and forth between two points (e.g., $f(x) = x^3 - 5x = 0$ near $x_0 = 1$).
  3. Poor Starting Point: If $x_0$ is chosen too far from root $\alpha$, the method may diverge or find an unintended distant root.
5. Descartes' Rule of Signs
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2021 (Predicting maximum positive, negative, and imaginary roots)
⚡ Essential Pass Content

Theorem Statement:

  • Positive Real Roots: The number of positive real roots of a polynomial equation $P(x) = 0$ cannot exceed the number of sign variations between consecutive non-zero coefficients of $P(x)$, and differs from it by an even integer.
  • Negative Real Roots: The number of negative real roots cannot exceed the number of sign variations in $P(-x)$, and differs from it by an even integer.
📝 Worked Exam Example:
Analyze roots of: $P(x) = x^4 - 3x^3 - 5x^2 + 2x - 1 = 0$ (Degree $n = 4$).

1. Check Positive Roots ($P(x)$):
Coefficients: $+1, -3, -5, +2, -1$
Signs: $(+) \to (-) \to (-) \to (+) \to (-)$
Sign changes:

  • Change 1: $+1$ to $-3$
  • Change 2: $-5$ to $+2$
  • Change 3: $+2$ to $-1$
Total sign changes = $3$. Therefore, positive real roots are either **3 or 1**.

2. Check Negative Roots ($P(-x)$):
Substitute $-x$:
$P(-x) = (-x)^4 - 3(-x)^3 - 5(-x)^2 + 2(-x) - 1 = x^4 + 3x^3 - 5x^2 - 2x - 1$
Signs: $(+) \to (+) \to (-) \to (-) \to (-)$
Sign change: only 1 change (from $+3$ to $-5$).
Therefore, negative real roots = **exactly 1**.

3. Complex Roots Deduction:
Since total roots = $4$: If positive = 3, negative = 1 $\implies$ complex = $4 - (3+1) = \mathbf{0}$.
If positive = 1, negative = 1 $\implies$ complex = $4 - (1+1) = \mathbf{2}$.
