Unit II: Interpolation & Extrapolation

Finite Differences (Δ, ∇, E), Operator Relations, Newton's Forward & Backward Interpolation, Divided Differences, and Lagrange's Formula.

⚡ Essential Operator & Interpolation Formulae
1. Key Operator Relationships:
• $\Delta = E - 1 \iff E = 1 + \Delta$
• $\nabla = 1 - E^{-1} \iff E^{-1} = 1 - \nabla$
• $\Delta = E\nabla = \nabla E$
• $E = e^{hD} \implies D = \frac{1}{h} \ln(1 + \Delta) = -\frac{1}{h} \ln(1 - \nabla)$
2. Newton's Forward Interpolation ($u = \frac{x - x_0}{h}$):
$$y = y_0 + u \Delta y_0 + \frac{u(u-1)}{2!} \Delta^2 y_0 + \frac{u(u-1)(u-2)}{3!} \Delta^3 y_0 + \dots$$ (Used when $x$ is near the beginning of table)
3. Newton's Backward Interpolation ($u = \frac{x - x_n}{h}$):
$$y = y_n + u \nabla y_n + \frac{u(u+1)}{2!} \nabla^2 y_n + \frac{u(u+1)(u+2)}{3!} \nabla^3 y_n + \dots$$ (Used when $x$ is near the end of table)
4. Lagrange's Interpolation Formula (Unequal Intervals):
$$y(x) = \frac{(x-x_1)(x-x_2)}{(x_0-x_1)(x_0-x_2)} y_0 + \frac{(x-x_0)(x-x_2)}{(x_1-x_0)(x_1-x_2)} y_1 + \frac{(x-x_0)(x-x_1)}{(x_2-x_0)(x_2-x_1)} y_2$$
1. Finite Differences & Operator Proofs
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24 (Prove $E = 1 + \Delta$, $E = e^{hD}$, and $\Delta = E\nabla$)
Operator Symbol Mathematical Definition
Forward Difference $\Delta$ $\Delta f(x) = f(x+h) - f(x)$
Backward Difference $\nabla$ $\nabla f(x) = f(x) - f(x-h)$
Shift Operator $E$ $E f(x) = f(x+h)$
Differential Operator $D$ $D f(x) = \frac{d}{dx} f(x)$
📐 Standard Proof: Prove that $E = e^{hD}$

By definition of Shift Operator:

E f(x) = f(x + h)

Expanding $f(x + h)$ by Taylor Series about $x$:

f(x + h) = f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2!} f''(x) + \frac{h^3}{3!} f'''(x) + \dots

Since $f'(x) = D f(x)$, $f''(x) = D^2 f(x)$, etc.:

f(x + h) = \left[ 1 + hD + \frac{(hD)^2}{2!} + \frac{(hD)^3}{3!} + \dots \right] f(x)

The expression in brackets is the expansion of exponential $e^{hD}$:

$$E f(x) = e^{hD} f(x) \implies E = e^{hD}$$

Taking natural logarithm on both sides:

$\ln E = hD \implies D = \frac{1}{h} \ln E$. Since $E = 1 + \Delta$:

$$D = \frac{1}{h} \ln(1 + \Delta) = \frac{1}{h} \left[ \Delta - \frac{\Delta^2}{2} + \frac{\Delta^3}{3} - \frac{\Delta^4}{4} + \dots \right]$$
2. Solved PYQ: Newton's Backward Interpolation
⭐ CSJMU PYQ 2025–26: Estimate population in 2017 from the given census data.
Year ($x$) Population ($y$ in '000) $\nabla y$ $\nabla^2 y$ $\nabla^3 y$
2010 45 - - -
2012 55 $55 - 45 = \mathbf{10}$ - -
2014 68 $68 - 55 = \mathbf{13}$ $13 - 10 = \mathbf{3}$ -
2016 83 $83 - 68 = \mathbf{15}$ $15 - 13 = \mathbf{2}$ $2 - 3 = \mathbf{-1}$
Step-by-Step Calculation:
Since $x = 2017$ is near the end, use Newton's Backward Formula with $x_n = 2016$, $y_n = 83$, and step size $h = 2$:
$$u = \frac{x - x_n}{h} = \frac{2017 - 2016}{2} = 0.5$$ $$y(2017) = y_n + u \nabla y_n + \frac{u(u+1)}{2!} \nabla^2 y_n + \frac{u(u+1)(u+2)}{3!} \nabla^3 y_n$$ $$y(2017) = 83 + (0.5)(15) + \frac{0.5 \times 1.5}{2} (2) + \frac{0.5 \times 1.5 \times 2.5}{6} (-1)$$ $$y(2017) = 83 + 7.5 + 0.75 - 0.3125 = \mathbf{90.9375} \text{ thousand}$$

Result: Population in 2017 is approximately 90,938.

3. Lagrange's Interpolation Formula
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2025–26, 2024–25, 2023–24, 2021 (Unequally spaced intervals)

When to use: When the given independent variable $x$ values are NOT equally spaced (i.e. $x_1 - x_0 \ne x_2 - x_1$), Newton's forward/backward formulas cannot be used. Lagrange's formula is the premier method.

📝 Solved Example: Find $y(9)$ using Lagrange's Formula

Given data: $(5, 12)$, $(6, 13)$, $(11, 14)$

Here $x_0 = 5, y_0 = 12$; $x_1 = 6, y_1 = 13$; $x_2 = 11, y_2 = 14$. Notice intervals are $1$ and $5$ (unequal!).

We need $y$ at $x = 9$:

y(9) = \frac{(9-6)(9-11)}{(5-6)(5-11)} (12) + \frac{(9-5)(9-11)}{(6-5)(6-11)} (13) + \frac{(9-5)(9-6)}{(11-5)(11-6)} (14)

Term 1: $\frac{3 \times (-2)}{(-1) \times (-6)} (12) = \frac{-6}{6} \times 12 = -12$

Term 2: $\frac{4 \times (-2)}{1 \times (-5)} (13) = \frac{-8}{-5} \times 13 = 1.6 \times 13 = 20.8$

Term 3: $\frac{4 \times 3}{6 \times 5} (14) = \frac{12}{30} \times 14 = 0.4 \times 14 = 5.6$

$$y(9) = -12 + 20.8 + 5.6 = \mathbf{14.4}$$
4. Newton's Divided Difference Formula

First Divided Difference: $f[x_0, x_1] = \frac{f(x_1) - f(x_0)}{x_1 - x_0}$

Second Divided Difference: $f[x_0, x_1, x_2] = \frac{f[x_1, x_2] - f[x_0, x_1]}{x_2 - x_0}$

$$P(x) = f[x_0] + (x - x_0)f[x_0, x_1] + (x - x_0)(x - x_1)f[x_0, x_1, x_2] + \dots$$
