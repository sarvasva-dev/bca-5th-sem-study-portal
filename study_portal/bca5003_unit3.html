2. Multiplexing: FDM, TDM & WDM
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2023–24, 2020, 2018 (FDM vs TDM and optical WDM explanation)

Multiplexing combines multiple independent data signals onto a single high-capacity physical transmission link:

The 3 Primary Multiplexing Techniques:
  1. Frequency-Division Multiplexing (FDM): Analog technique. The link bandwidth is divided into non-overlapping frequency bands separated by Guard Bands (e.g. FM Radio, Cable TV).
  2. Time-Division Multiplexing (TDM): Digital technique. Devices share the entire bandwidth by taking turns in dedicated time slots:
    • Synchronous TDM: Each device is assigned a fixed time slot, even if it has no data (wastes slots).
    • Statistical TDM: Dynamically allocates slots only to active transmitting devices.
  3. Wavelength-Division Multiplexing (WDM): Optical equivalent of FDM. Multiple laser beams of different colors/wavelengths ($\lambda$) are combined onto a single optical fiber strand.

4. ISDN (Integrated Services Digital Network)
⭐ CSJMU PYQs: 2024–25, 2021 (ISDN concept, B-channel vs D-channel, BRI vs PRI)

ISDN provides end-to-end digital transmission of voice, video, and packet data over standard copper telephone lines.

ISDN Channels & Interfaces:
  • B-Channel (Bearer): $64 \text{ kbps}$ digital channel for carrying user voice, audio, and data.
  • D-Channel (Delta): $16 \text{ kbps}$ (in BRI) or $64 \text{ kbps}$ (in PRI) for call signaling, setup, and teardown.
  • Basic Rate Interface (BRI): Consists of $2B + D = 2(64) + 16 = \mathbf{144 \text{ kbps}}$ (suitable for residential and small business connections).
  • Primary Rate Interface (PRI):
    • North America / Japan: $23B + D = 23(64) + 64 = \mathbf{1.544 \text{ Mbps}}$ (T1 carrier).
    • Europe / International: $30B + 2D = 30(64) + 64 + 64 = \mathbf{2.048 \text{ Mbps}}$ (E1 carrier).
